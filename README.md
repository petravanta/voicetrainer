# VoiceTrainer

A browser-based voice training app for tracking **resonance** and **pitch**, aimed at people working toward a more feminine (or masculine) voice. In the vein of voiceduck.cc.

## Modes

- **Readings**: read a prepared passage aloud; press Finish for a result card (resonance, /s/ brightness, pitch, breathiness, change since last time) and the weakest vowels to practise. **Your vowels** maps every vowel you held steadily on the F1/F2 vowel chart against the typical masculine and feminine values, with its own score and the words it was heard in (consonants are left out). **Focus spots** pick the few short bits of the passage where getting to your goal gains the most (how far each word was from the goal, added up over the phrase); **Practise** opens just that bit in the reader, scores every try against the reading (a bar per try for how far it is from the goal, each word's number in the reading next to the latest try, and a plain-words verdict on which words reached the goal and which are still off), and saves the tries in Practice progress. **Timeline** is one tall view of the reading, every lane on the same clock, playhead and zoom: pitch dots coloured from darker to brighter resonance, the resonance line (smoothed over a quarter second) against the goal band, a spectrogram of the recording (0-5 kHz, with the F1-F3 read marked), and each word across its own stretch of time with its pitch as a level and its resonance as a bar (so words are not evenly spaced). While you read, the highlight moves a word on for each syllable it hears, which can lump two words together when it misses a syllable or jumps at a pause; so once you press Finish, each word's edges are found again in the recording: each word's spelling becomes a short chain of sound classes (vowel-like, hiss, other consonant sound, stop), and the whole reading is matched against the kind of sound in each moment (voiced, hiss, other, quiet), with pauses and stray noise (a cough, an "um", background hiss) given their own catch-all so they can't take words. How far it may go is capped a little past the live highlight's last word (or exactly at the last word Check words heard), and it finds where you stopped inside that. The timeline (and Word by word, see below) uses these edges. Checked against Whisper word timings on two real readings: median about 0.1-0.14 s off, 85-90% of words within 0.3 s; on the demo voice about 20 ms, the neural voice about 90 ms. Limits: the classes come from spelling, not a dictionary of sounds, so silent letters and unusual spellings can nudge an edge; if the live highlight fell far behind (more than about 15%), words past the cap are left as not reached; and it can credit a word or two too many at the very end. Readings saved before this keep the edges they were saved with. Zoom with − / Fit / +, a two-finger pinch, or Ctrl + wheel; lane names stay put while it scrolls sideways. The recording of your last 10 readings stays in this browser (IndexedDB; never synced or uploaded, deleted with the reading): tap or click anywhere on the timeline to cue that moment (a cue line across every lane, and under the timeline its time, pitch, resonance, F1-F3, the word there and, when a reference is laid over it, the reference's pitch and resonance), then Play / Stop plays from the cue (the playhead follows and scrolls along; Stop keeps the cue). Drag with a mouse to scrub the cue; on a phone drag the cue's marker with a finger (a finger anywhere else still scrolls the timeline); arrow keys nudge it and Space plays or stops. Tapping a word cues it and shows its numbers. When a reading has a timeline, the older **Word by word** chart is put away (it said the same things) and a focus spot's Show jumps to the spot on the timeline; readings with no timeline on this device (older than the last 10, or from another device) still show Word by word, where a picked word can be heard with Hear this word when the recording is there. History's Report brings the same timeline back. **Check words** (reader button, optional and online, same consent as Show words) has the browser's speech recognition write down what you said and lines it up with the passage, so passage words you skipped are marked *possibly missed* (dashed box) in the Timeline (or Word by word). A word heard as something else is not marked, and words after the last one heard count as not reached. Without it the app only hears your voice, which can't tell a skipped word from one the syllable-following highlight lost track of, so a word the highlight passed with no voice on it just says so when you tap it. The History panel lists the spots that keep coming up over your last five readings. Each reading is saved with one trend per passage; readings under 70% of the text are kept as partial and left out of the trend.
- **Freestyle**: the resonance meter, a **Timeline** with pitch, resonance and the spectrogram stacked on one clock (pitch dots coloured from darker to brighter resonance, the smoothed resonance line against the goal band, F1-F3 over the spectrogram, a faint line each second), the vowel space and the vocal tract sketch, and a vowel map of the session that fills in as you talk. When you stop the microphone, or a recording or the demo voice ends, the take opens on the same tall timeline as a reading's: tap anywhere to cue a moment and see its numbers, Play / Stop from the cue, zoom, and Save audio for a microphone take. Nothing is saved; the next take replaces it (the first ten minutes of a take are kept). On a phone the vowel space and vocal tract sit side by side and you swipe between them.
- **Sing along**: play a song anywhere (Spotify, YouTube, a CD) with headphones on, or attach a karaoke video, and sing. Search for the lyrics by artist and title (from [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net), a free third-party lyrics library that has timed versions; when it's busy, unreachable or finds nothing, the app retries once and then asks [lyrics.ovh](https://lyrics.ovh), which has plain lyrics only and looks titles up on Deezer; each result says where it came from and whether it's timed, and a link opens a web search for the lyrics when both fail. Only what you type is sent; lyrics you load are saved (with your other data, so in your Google Drive once connected, and on every device you use it from) and listed under Your songs at the top of the Sing tab, sorted by last used (newest first), song name or artist (Sort by; the choice is remembered in this browser), each saying when it was last used (picking a song or starting to sing it counts), with the loaded one marked and filtered by what you type in the search box, so you can sing them again without searching or offline, with × to remove one and a red Delete all saved songs button under the list to clear it, each undoable; the list keeps up to 40 songs), or tap **New song** to type or paste them yourself, giving the song a name and an artist (both are required before the lyrics can be used, and the song is kept in Your songs under that name). The lyrics box stays hidden until you pick a song or start a new one. Lyrics can be plain text (press Next line or the space bar as you go) or as timed LRC lyrics (they scroll by themselves: with a song file they follow it exactly, otherwise press Next line as the first line begins, then nudge by half a second if they drift). **Follow my singing** (optional) moves through plain lyrics by itself: it counts the syllables you sing (voiced onsets and steps to a new note) against each line's rough syllable count and shows the next line when you pause after most of a line; with timed lyrics and no song file it starts them on your first phrase and lets the start of each phrase nudge them back in step. It's approximate: room sound never moves it, but long held notes over many words, one word over many notes, or music from speakers can throw it off, and Next line, Back a line (Backspace) or a tap on a line puts it right. You see the note you're singing, your resonance and a pitch trace coloured by resonance; each line gets its resonance and range as you finish it, and Finish shows a summary per line plus **Notes you held**: a chart of seconds spent holding each semitone steady (0.25 s or longer; slides, scoops and passing notes left out) against your goal's speaking range, with each note's resonance on tap. Your singing isn't saved. Music from speakers mixes into the microphone, so use headphones; with a song file there's an echo cancellation option for speakers. Pitch is read up to about 1100 Hz (C6) while singing (500 Hz elsewhere). **Target tune** (optional): write a line's notes after it (or on the row under it) in braces, like `{C4 C4 D#4 C4 G3}`; while you sing that line the notes show as bands on the pitch trace and the note readout says when you're on one, and the summary gives the share of held singing on the tune per line and overall, separately counting notes an octave away. It checks which notes you held, not their timing. The app can't hook into Spotify itself: browsers can't capture Spotify's audio, and its API has no lyrics.
  - **Attach video** (optional): karaoke and instrumental versions of songs are licensed, so none are bundled or downloaded by the app. Instead you can paste a YouTube link and tap **Attach video** (for example a karaoke video found with the **Find a karaoke version on YouTube** link, which searches for the open song's artist and title); it plays in YouTube's own embedded player (youtube-nocookie.com) at the top of the Sing tab, and timed lyrics follow the video's position. Some videos can only be played on YouTube itself, and the app says so. Or use an instrumental or karaoke file from your device. An attached video is saved as part of the song's entry in Your songs (only the link, start time and lyric timing), so it comes back when you pick the song again and is removed with the song; attaching a video to a song that isn't in Your songs yet saves the song (a New song needs its name and artist first, and until then the video stays with the open song across reloads). A song file is kept in this browser's IndexedDB. Your songs marks songs with "video attached" or "song file". Each song has one track, the video or the file, and the lyric timing you set against it with Next line or the half-second nudges is remembered. YouTube's player is only loaded once you use a video and open the Sing tab.
- **Practice**: one exercise at a time with the live coach. Every finished exercise is saved, with last, best and a trend line per exercise. The tabs come in two groups. **Sounds** score one sound at a time against typical voices for that exact sound: **Vowels**, **Sibilants** (the held hisses plus the hiss brightness exercises) and **Words** (a hiss into a vowel). **Skills** train something across sounds: **Resonance** (moving the whole tract on purpose: the dark-to-bright slide and the whispers), **Control** (one part at a time, to see which reading follows it), **Breath** (breathiness, with the dog panting warm-up) and **Expression** (intonation patterns and the embodied-voice game). A one-line note under the tabs says what each tab trains, with more behind its info badge. An exercise's history follows its id, so moving it to another tab keeps its history.

Phones get a tab bar along the bottom; the screen stays awake while the microphone runs.

## Microphone and voice check

Settings has a 15-second check for when readings come back mostly empty: 2 seconds of quiet, a held "ah" and "ee", then one sentence. It names the most likely limit: a microphone that only passes phone-call sound (a Bluetooth headset drops to this while its mic is on; found from the highest frequency the voice reaches 10 dB above the room and within 60 dB of its peak, about 8–12 kHz for a normal mic and 3.5–4.5 kHz for a phone line), clipping, background noise, processing that cuts the room to digital silence, a quiet signal, a voice too airy to read, or held vowels the app can't read. Nasality isn't checked: on test voices a nasal resonance didn't show up reliably enough to name. Nothing is saved or sent.

## Check my readings

Also in Settings: a self-paced self-test of whether the readings follow what you do. You hold one vowel ("ah", "ee" or "oo") eight ways: dark, everyday and bright on one note. Each step records from your first sound and coaches you live (off your first note, not brighter than dark yet, not 4 notes down or 5 up (about 7 is the aim), not airy enough, no steady reading), then waits for Next (Next anyway and Redo are always there); the last 4 s of each step are analysed; everyday on a lower and a higher note; everyday again; very airy; whispered. Each step is read the way an exercise reads a held vowel, against the vowel you were asked for, and four checks come out in plain words: does the resonance score follow your change (bright at least 8 points over dark, everyday in between), does pitch alone move it (under 8 points across the notes; a bigger move passes when F2 and F3 moved with it, since that is your resonance changing with the note, which is common: the score holds still within a few points from 85 to 240 Hz on a voice whose formants don't move), does the same sound repeat (within 5), and does breathiness follow your air (airy at least 15 over everyday, or 8 over with the tone softer, H1-H2 up 4 dB on the same note; the whisper read as air). A step with too few readings is named instead of judged. The app can't know how dark or bright you really were, so the result shows F2 and F3 next to the score: if the formants moved and the score didn't, the app fell short; if neither moved, the change was mostly pitch, loudness or tone. A table lists each step's pitch, score, F1–F3 and breathiness, and the recording can be saved as WAV to check in [Praat](https://www.praat.org) (Formant listing; maximum formant 5000 Hz for a lower voice, 5500 Hz for a higher one). "Check it another way" lists outside checks: Praat, another voice, the synthetic demo voices, repeating on another day or microphone, and a listener.

## Saved data and themes

Readings, practice history, settings, your own texts and Your songs (lyrics plus attached video links; song files stay in the browser) live in one file (`VoiceTrainer data.json`) in your own Google Drive, using the shared sign-in of [petravanta's apps](https://github.com/petravanta/general/blob/main/STANDARDS.md) (drive.file scope: the app only sees the file it creates). Until Drive is connected, or when the app runs from a file or a claude.ai Artifact, the browser keeps them. The Settings tab holds the goal (feminine or masculine; every target follows it), your own target pitch range per goal (or the typical band, with a reset), and the theme (system, light or dark). The theme is shared with the switch in the header and with petravanta's other apps on this device. **Spectrogram colours** picks how every spectrogram is coloured (Freestyle live and its timeline, reading timelines, the demo voice's): Classic (follows the light or dark theme), Rainbow (a nod to the Rainbow Passage), Colour-blind safe (viridis), Ember and Mono, with a small sample to preview them. It is saved with the other settings, so it syncs through Drive.

## Features

- **Resonance scoring** per vowel on a 0–100 scale (average man 37.5, average woman 62.5, room past both), comparing measured formants F1–F3 against Hillenbrand et al. (1995) male/female averages for the same vowel, with target bands where 90% of real speakers sit.
- **Consonant feedback**: sibilant (s/sh) spectral centre of gravity.
- **Big dog, little dog** (Practice > Resonance): the classic resonance contrast, scored. Hold a deep, dark "huh" (big dog: larynx low, throat wide), pause, then a small, bright "huh" (little dog: larynx up, space small) on the same note, twice. Each hold is read against "uh"'s typical formants however dark or bright it is; in each pair the darker hold is the big dog. A pair counts when the two are at least 12 points apart on the resonance score and within 2 semitones in pitch; the score kept is your goal's end (little dog for a feminine goal, big dog for a masculine one). Rows show the hold now, the big dogs and the little dogs against the goal band, with Target vs you (and Sustain) on the goal's end. The unscored panting warm-up stays in Breath as Dog panting.
- **Resonance chains** (Practice > Resonance): a vowel chain on one breath (ee – ay – ah – oh – oo), a word chain (me, may, ma, mow, moo) and a phrase chain ("Lee may walk home soon.", "Eve made all those shoes."). Each steady moment is matched to one of the chain's vowels, the way a reading matches a word's vowels, so every vowel gets its own score and bar; a take is done once all five have one, and the score kept is the middle of them, with the vowel furthest from the goal named. They sweep from front to back vowels, where resonance usually slips. Demo sounds for both are graded by the same checks in Check the checker. Checked on synthetic voices only (classic and natural); `data/reso.json` holds them.
- **Hiss brightness exercises** (Practice > Sibilants, after the sustained hisses): an "sss" to "shh" glide that scores the "shh" end of each slide (find the most forward "shh"), a lip ladder for "shh" and a teeth ladder for "sss" (one held hiss per step, rounded to smile or open to teeth together, reversed for a masculine goal), and s/sh word pairs and phrases where every hiss is sorted into /s/ or /ʃ/ and scored. Live tips per hiss, demo sounds graded by the same checks, Target vs you (with Sustain) on the ladders and pairs. Hiss bands reuse the resonance bands, with no data of their own behind them, so treat them as a direction. Checked on synthetic hisses only.
- **Pitch tracking** (YIN), with optional words over the pitch track ("Show words"): words appear faded as they are recognised and firm up when final (browser speech recognition; in Chrome and Edge that audio is sent to Google or Microsoft, so it is off until you agree to that in a consent prompt, and you can withdraw consent at any time). In reading practice the words come from the passage as the highlight moves; recognition runs there only for Check words. In Freestyle, when you stop the microphone the words heard during the take also go along the take's Timeline ("Words heard" lanes), each over its stretch of voice with its own pitch and resonance; tap one to cue it and see its numbers. The Web Speech API gives no word timings, so each final result's words are spread over the voice heard since the previous result (one word per voiced stretch when the counts match, otherwise by syllables), which can put a word a word or so off; low-confidence words (below 0.6, when the browser gives a confidence) and words that never became final are dotted as unsure. Works in Chrome, Edge and Safari (not Firefox), microphone only, online.
- **Control exercises**: move only the jaw, tongue, lips or larynx (or only the pitch) and see that just the matching formant moves, to build trust in the feedback. The resonance slide (listed under Practice > Resonance) goes from dark to bright on one note and checks that the pitch holds while the resonance moves.
- **Whisper exercises** (Practice > Resonance): a whisper siren (slide the whisper's brightness up and down twice) and a whispered "kee" held in the goal zone. With no voice, pitch is out of the way; the formants are read from the whisper's airflow noise (LPC, F2 and F3 only, after allowing for the usual upward shift of whispered formants), so the score is a trend within one setup rather than an exact match to voiced vowels.
- **Embody** (Practice > Expression, "Uwu with feeling"): an unscored thought exercise in embodied voice. Say "uwu" ("eww-woo") in your goal voice while conveying a feeling dealt at random from 75 (smug, sleepy, bashful, indignant, wistful...), each with a one-line scene; "Another feeling" deals a new one from a different family and never one of your last 20. A gentle live view shows pitch across and resonance up with a shaded home zone, each take leaves a mark named after its feeling, and after each take a question asks what your jaw, lips, breath, face or throat did. Nothing is scored or saved to history.
- **Breathiness** estimate (0–100) from cepstral peak prominence (CPP, 29 dB reads 0 and 13 dB reads 100; until 2026-11 the clear end was 25 dB, which read every clear voice and a mildly airy one alike as 0, and saved values are moved to the new scale), read only when the ~85 ms window is evenly voiced (a window that catches a vowel's start or a consonant read as airy: one real voice's sentence read 39 against 0–10 for its held vowels, 19 with this rule); the display uses the last ~1.5 s only and says "mostly air" when there is too little tone to read, instead of holding on to older clear readings. Calibrated on synthetic voices, so read it as a trend within one setup. With breath exercises (Big dog / little dog, sigh to clear tone, whisper to voice, clean onset, hum into a vowel, breathy-clear-pressed, straw or lip-trill siren warm-up) and a live gauge.
- **Prosody exercises** (Practice > Expression): question rise, settled statement, statement-then-question, expressive range, stressing one word and melody vs monotone. Each spoken phrase is checked from its pitch contour in semitones around your own middle pitch, with a synthesized example for each.
- **Check the checker**: every exercise shows demo sounds that should pass and should not (typical feminine vs masculine, right vs wrong movement, whispered vs flat or voiced, clear vs breathy vs pressed, intended vs flat intonation), each graded by exactly the same scoring as your voice. While a demo plays, the live coach, gauges and vocal tract sketch show where the demo voice's jaw, tongue, lips and larynx likely sit (and the breath, control and prosody panels show its reading), without scoring it as you. Every other piece of feedback (the resonance meter text, the main vocal tract sketch, the exercise message) says when it is showing a demo or reading voice, and earlier tips such as "that sounds closer to..." are cleared when a demo starts.
- **Guided exercises** built from your weakest vowels and sibilants, with synthesized reference examples and before/after scores.
- **Live spectrogram** with goal markers for the current vowel.
- **Target vs you**: in the Vowels, Sibilants, Words, Resonance and Control exercises, and in a Readings focus spot's Practise, two short spectrograms sit side by side on one frequency scale and one time scale: the target on the left (the exercise's own demo sound, at typical values for your goal; for a focus spot, the reference voice, neural or demo, reading just those words) and you on the right, scrolling in live and then frozen on the stretch that was scored (or your latest try). Both are made from the analyzer's frames the same way, with F1-F3 marked (and the centre of a hiss); for a single sound, dashed lines at your goal's F1-F3 (or hiss centre) run across both, so a band of yours under its line is darker than the goal. Under it, the formant that differs most, in Hz and percent, with the others in brackets. **Hear target** plays the target. **Sustain** (next to it, kept on this device) makes Hear target hold the sound instead: a vowel, a hiss, a word's vowel (after its hiss) or a whispered vowel at one steady pitch and the same goal formants, on a seamless loop, until you press Stop (or after two minutes), in both the Natural and Classic voice. Like every demo it isn't scored as you while it plays. **Your take** (exercises only): after a take you're happy with, **☆ Keep this take** saves the sound of its scored stretch as a third reference, beside the demo sound and the goal lines. A **Typical / Demo voice / Your take** switch picks the left picture (remembered on this device): Typical is the exercise's sound at typical values for your goal; Demo voice is the same sound made by the demo voice set in Settings, at its pitch, resonance and brightness (Sustain holds it too); Your take is the kept take. Hear plays whichever is shown and the line underneath compares your try with it; the goal lines and the score don't change. Control's movements have no demo-voice version. **Check the checker** in those exercises also grades **Your demo voice** and, once you've kept one, **Your kept take**, each with its score, F1-F3 and a play button. One per exercise and goal (keeping another replaces it, ✕ forgets it), kept in this browser only (IndexedDB) and never synced, since it is your voice. Sounds that move (the whisper siren, Control's movements) have no held version. Breath, Prosody and Embody have no resonance target and don't get it; neither do Freestyle and Sing (no target sound) nor the full reading timeline (its dashed reference lines already compare). Checked on synthetic voices only.
- **Reference spectrogram** (Readings, optional, off by default): with Compare with the reference on, a **Reference spectrogram** button on the reading's timeline adds a lane under your spectrogram with the reference voice's (neural or demo) spectrogram and its F1-F3, each word stretched (0.4-2.5x) onto how long you took over it, blank between words and where you have no word. The choice is remembered in this browser. Checked on synthetic and neural-voice audio only.
- **Live drill coach**: one concrete adjustment at a time (jaw, tongue, lips/larynx) with per-formant gauges.
- **Formant by formant**: F1 (jaw), F2 (tongue) and F3 (lips, larynx) each on the resonance scale with its goal zone, and how far and which way each must move (in % and Hz), live in Freestyle and the vowel and word drills, and pooled over the vowels in reading results, History reports, drill results and the Freestyle session (with the formant furthest off called out, and F2 and F3 low together read as throat length). **Focus** (All, F1, F2, F3 or Worst) narrows the bars and the coach's tip to one formant so you can dial them in one at a time. F1 is set aside at high pitch, marked "?" when it alone disagrees with F2 and F3, and a formant outside anything a voice does reads "couldn't measure". Zones are the live coach's (`D.formantOffsets`, `test/formants.test.js`).
- **Vocal tract sketch**: a side view of one plausible tract shape for your formants (jaw from F1, tongue from F2, lips from F3, larynx height from the shared shift), with the goal shape and the recommended change drawn on top.
- **Reading practice**: the reader behind Readings, with the highlight following your voice, a single-line focus track, steady-pace and tap modes, and your own texts.
- **Demo voice settings**: voice type, pitch, resonance and brightness for the synthetic demo voice; type, pitch and speed for the reading voice.
- **Demo voice as a reading reference**: passages can be read by the demo voice (the default) or the browser's speech voice. The demo voice is made in the app, word by word with the vowels from the pronunciation dictionary, so its timing is known exactly and the app can measure it: the meters follow it live, **Demo voice** on each passage in Readings shows its measured pitch, resonance, vowel map and timeline (playable, not saved), and **Compare with the demo voice** on your own reading result draws it as dashed lines on the timeline, word by word and the vowel map, stretched word by word onto your own pace. It is a machine voice with simple melody and rough consonants; it's a target and a check on the measuring, not a model speaker.
- **Natural or Classic practice voice** (in Demo voice settings, Natural by default): the natural voice uses a Liljencrants-Fant glottal pulse (one Rd number for pressed to breathy; Fant 1995) with cycle-to-cycle jitter (~0.6%) and shimmer (~4%), airflow noise strongest while the folds are open, pitch that settles in and wanders, formants that drift under 1% and glide through consonants, voiced sounds in a word said as one run instead of joined pieces, and a soft room on the speakers (the charts see it dry). With it, Play demo voice says "see, spa, sue, said" as a spoken list (each vowel gliding in from the s or p, pitch rising on each word and falling on the last) instead of an s and four held vowels, and the masculine-to-feminine sweep says the list three times; `test/examples.test.js` checks each word reads as its vowel at the set pitch and resonance. The F1–F3 targets are the same as the classic voice, and `test/natural.test.js` reruns the analyzer tests with it: across the 12 vowels at both goals the measured formants are within 3.6% / 1.3% / 1.3% of target (classic 2.7% / 1.0% / 1.2%).

Everything runs in the browser; no audio leaves your machine (except the optional Show words and Check words recognition noted above). The Sing along lyrics search sends only the typed artist and title to LRCLIB and, as a backup, lyrics.ovh (and to DuckDuckGo if you use the web search link); lyrics you load stay in your browser's local storage and are never uploaded; no lyrics are bundled with the app. An attached YouTube video is played by YouTube's embedded player, so YouTube receives that request; song files stay in your browser.

## Usage

```sh
node build.js            # writes dist/VoiceTrainer.html (standalone) and dist/artifact.html
```

Open `dist/VoiceTrainer.html` in a browser and allow microphone access, or use the hosted version above. Every push to `main` rebuilds and redeploys the hosted version via GitHub Actions.

The footer shows which build you are running (commit and build time in your own time zone with UTC alongside, or `dev build` for the unbuilt source); tap it to copy the id into a bug report. The hosted page checks `version.json` (never from cache) when it opens or comes back to the front, and offers a reload when a newer deploy is out.

```sh
node test/build.test.js     # build stamp: commit and time reach the page, footer label, dev fallback, update check

node test/tvy.test.js       # Target vs you: strips from analyzer frames mark F1-F3 and a hiss's centre where they were set, trim silence, and name the biggest gap

```

## The neural voice

Each built-in reading passage comes with two pre-made readings by a neural text-to-speech voice ([Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M), Apache-2.0): `af_heart` (feminine) and `am_puck` (masculine), in [`refclips/`](refclips/) as MP3 (about 3 MB for all of them, each loaded only when used). The model gives each phoneme's length, so each word's start and end in a clip is known, not guessed. The app plays them as the reading voice and measures them exactly as it measures you, as the dashed reference on your readings. Your own passages, and copies of the page that cannot load the clips (the standalone file, the Artifact), use the synthetic demo voice instead.

Measured by the app (`test/refclips.test.js`): the feminine voice reads 196-223 Hz and resonance 55-70 across the passages, the masculine one 108-132 Hz and resonance 38-47 (the top of the masculine band). To remake them after changing a passage, or with other voices:

```
pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile onnx      # and ffmpeg on PATH
# kokoro-v1.0.onnx and voices-v1.0.bin from https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/tag/model-files-v1.0
python3 scripts/make-refclips.py --model <folder with those two files> [--only b-rainbow] [--fem af_heart] [--masc am_puck]
```

A passage changed without remaking its clips falls back to the demo voice (the test warns).

## How the resonance score works

1. **Pitch**: YIN (de Cheveigné & Kawahara 2002) on about 65 ms of audio.
2. **Formants**: the audio is resampled to about 11 kHz, pre-emphasised, windowed (30 ms Hamming) and fitted with a 10th-order linear prediction (LPC) model; the roots of that model give F1, F2 and F3, the three lowest resonances of the vocal tract. A median of the last five frames steadies them. A frame is scored only when the last frames agree: within 6% for a held drill vowel, within 10% in running speech (readings, freestyle, singing), whose vowels last only 80–150 ms and glide into their consonants. Poles up to 700 Hz wide count as formants (a higher voice's are wider). On eight clips of a real woman reading ([LJSpeech](https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/), public domain) this scores 18% of voiced frames instead of 8%, with the same score on synthetic voices. In a reading, a frame too short to hold still is also read on its own (unsmoothed); a word with no steady frames gets a rough score (pale bar) from 3 such frames, and these only fill in the overall score when steady frames are under 20. On the same clips this raises content words with a score from 65% to 76% with the same word accuracy on synthetic speech; shorter LPC windows, 3-frame smoothing, a pitch-scaled lag window and re-picking formants from all candidates gained 0–4 points and were not kept. A word with no reading shows a struck-through ring under the axis, never an empty or low bar. A reading's score is the median of all its scored frames; under 20 it's marked ≈ (rough), under 6 it shows why there is none (too little voice, too high, vowels that couldn't be measured, or none held still). On synthetic test vowels this agrees with Praat's Burg analysis within a few points.
3. **Vowel**: F1–F3 are matched against the 12 American English vowels of [Hillenbrand et al. (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.411872). In Read mode the vowels each word should have are tried first; a clear vowel that isn't one of them (the highlight is often a syllable off) still counts for the word and the overall score, but not per vowel.
4. **Score**: on a log-frequency scale, F1–F3 are projected onto the line from the average man's values (alpha 0) to the average woman's (alpha 1) for that vowel, weighted 0.6 / 1 / 0.8. Nothing is normalised for speaker size, because size and shape are what the score measures. The number shown is `25 × (alpha + 1.5)`: 0 and 100 are the edges of what an adult voice plausibly measures (alpha −1.5 and 2.5), the average man is 37.5 and the average woman 62.5, so no real voice is pinned at an end.
5. **Target bands**: masculine 28–45, feminine 52–75. They are where 90% of real speakers sit: in Hillenbrand et al.'s own recordings (45 men, 48 women; the [phonTools](https://cran.r-project.org/package=phonTools) copy of the data), each speaker's median alpha across their vowels has 5th–95th percentiles of −0.31 to 0.27 for the men and 0.60 to 1.46 for the women, rounded out to −0.35..0.3 and 0.6..1.5. Brighter than the feminine band (or darker than the masculine one) still counts as on target. The older Peterson & Barney (1952) recordings sit 6–10 points darker for both groups, so the edges are soft: the bands describe one dialect and decade. The sibilant score uses the same scale and bands without that evidence behind it. `test/scale.test.js` checks the bands against those speakers and synthetic voices.

Scores saved before November 2026 were `alpha × 100` clamped to 0–100. They are converted when the data loads (`D.migrateRecord`; old 0 and 100 become 37.5 and 62.5, since the real value was there or further out) and drawn as hollow dots in a passage's history.

**Limits.** The 25 points between the two averages are a 10–20% formant change, so a 1% measuring error moves the score 1–2 points; the meter shows a rough ± based on the F0/4 accuracy rule of thumb ([Kent & Vorperian 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6002811/)). In a higher voice LPC pulls F1 towards a harmonic ([Shadle, Nam & Whalen 2016](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4940665)), which made a male-sized tract score about 40 on "ee" at 220 Hz; so once pitch passes half a vowel's typical F1 (about 195–210 Hz for "ee" and "oo"; 45%, about 175–190 Hz, before the first-harmonic fix below was measured: from 2x down to 1.75x the pitch the full fit stayed within 2–3 points like F2 and F3 alone, under 1.75x it was 5–18 off) the score uses F2 and F3 only. At any pitch, a frame whose three formants don't fit the vowel but whose F2 and F3 agree closely (within 0.06 on the fit) is also read from F2 and F3: one real woman's held "ee" had F1 near 240 Hz, below both references, and was refused 91% of the time though F2 and F3 were clearly feminine. When no pole narrower than 700 Hz sits below 1100 Hz, the clearest wider pole (up to 1200 Hz wide) is taken as F1 instead of letting F2 slide into its place; the same voice's "ah" had an F1 about 630–830 Hz wide (Praat agrees). Together these took her held vowels from 9% to 53% read, and her "She sells sea shells" sentence from 17 to 58 scored frames, with synthetic accuracy unchanged and the eight LJSpeech clips from 76% to 81% of content words. In running speech LPC also often puts a pole on the pitch itself (on one real 175 Hz reading of the Rainbow Passage, half the frames had "F1" at 1.1–1.3× the pitch), so an F1 under 1.5× the pitch is set aside too and the vowel is identified and scored from F2 and F3; on that reading this took scored moments from 27 to 73 and words with a bar from 9 to 14 of the 25 content words reached. That pole on the pitch comes from a first harmonic far stronger than the rest: on three real readings by one light, breathy-leaning voice the first harmonic stood about 19 dB over the second (median; a firm voice 0–8, the neural voice reading the same passage 5), and LPC spent a pole pair on it, leaving no pole at all for the real F1 in about 93% of those frames. So before LPC, a first harmonic more than 4 dB over the second is cut back to 4 dB over it (a peaking EQ at the pitch, Q 2; `D.tameH1`). On those readings F1 at the pitch went from 53–64% of steady frames to 0–3%, the median F1 from 220–250 Hz to 450–570 Hz (the neural voice: 490–500 Hz), and that voice's held "ee" from about 220 to 265–320 Hz at 155–180 Hz pitch (fitting her harmonics with the synthetic voice puts the true F1 at about 260–300 Hz). On synthetic voices a breathy source's F1 error went from 27% to 7% (it had read 26% low on average), with firm voices unchanged (`test/h1.test.js`). From 200 Hz of pitch up, a closed vowel's F1 can sit on the first harmonic itself, and the cut took it too: a held "ee" with F1 near 300 Hz read 450–650 Hz. So above 200 Hz the cut is only kept when the plain reading had F1 on the pitch and the cut finds one at 2.5× the pitch or more (an open vowel); a 300 Hz "ee" F1 at 250–320 Hz now reads 260–335. Where F1 is set aside, the app says which rule and the numbers ("set aside: pitch 215 Hz (over 193)"), and Target vs you never names a set-aside F1 as the biggest difference. In Freestyle and singing, where the vowel is recognised from sound, a vowel held steadily keeps its name while its resonance moves (as long as it still fits that vowel within 0.085): before, sliding a held vowel brighter or darker could flip its name mid-slide (a bright "eh" read as a dark "a", a bright "aw" as a dark "oh"), so the meter stalled or jumped by 20–30 points instead of following; on synthetic slides of every vowel the hold keeps the score rising steadily. Readings outside alpha −1.5 to 2.5 (usually a nasal resonance or a harmonic taken for a formant) are refused rather than shown as 0 or 100. The score has been tested on synthetic voices only, and the average is not a target: listeners rely most on pitch ([Leung, Oates & Chan 2018](https://doi.org/10.1044/2017_JSLHR-S-17-0067); [Gelfer & Mikos 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvoice.2004.10.006)). The same summary, with links, is under "How the resonance score works" at the bottom of the app.
